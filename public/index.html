03 / THE LIVES OF STARS

Born in darkness.
Made of stardust.

Stars are not eternal. Their mass determines how they shine, how long they live, and what remains when they die.

A COSMIC LIFE STORY

From a cloud of gas to a stellar remnant.

Gravity gathers gas and dust into a dense core. When the core becomes hot enough, nuclear fusion begins. A star then spends most of its life balancing gravity with pressure from its hot interior.

Its ending depends mostly on how much mass it was born with.

The ending

Lower-mass stars leave white dwarfs; massive stars may explode and leave neutron stars or black holes.

STELLAR REMNANT
◉

Lower- and medium-mass stars

They shed their outer layers, often creating a planetary nebula, and leave a hot, dense white dwarf that slowly cools.

✹

Massive stars

They can undergo a core-collapse supernova. The core may become a neutron star or, if massive enough, a black hole.

Stellar evolution varies with mass, composition, and interactions with companion stars. A supernova is not the fate of every star.
